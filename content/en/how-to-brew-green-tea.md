Almost everyone who says "I don't like green tea" actually drank badly brewed green tea. It is the easiest infusion to ruin and the fastest to turn bitter. The good news: two numbers fix it.

## The two numbers that matter

**Water temperature.** Green tea does not tolerate boiling water. At 100 °C the leaves cook and release tannins all at once — and tannins are exactly what tastes bitter. The right range is **70–80 °C**.

Without a thermometer: boil the water, pour it into another cup or pot, and wait. Two or three minutes brings it down from 100 °C to roughly 80 °C. If you own a kitchen thermometer, even better — it changes the result permanently.

**Steeping time.** Between **2 and 3 minutes**. Past that point the leaf keeps giving, and what it gives is bitterness. A green tea left for five minutes is a different drink.

| Green tea | Water | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Sencha | 70–80 °C | 1–2 min |
| Gunpowder | 75–80 °C | 2–3 min |
| Matcha (whisked) | 80 °C | whisk, do not steep |
| Jasmine green tea | 80 °C | 2–3 min |

## The ratio

One teaspoon of leaf per 200–250 ml cup. With whole leaf, the tea takes up volume — do not compress it. The leaf needs room to open, and that movement is what makes the infusion round.

## Re-steeping: the whole-leaf advantage

The clearest difference between a tea bag and loose leaf shows up in the second cup. Whole leaf handles **two or three more infusions**. The first gives you the most delicate and aromatic notes; the second, the deepest; the third, the softest. The same pinch of leaf makes three cups instead of one.

The fine, milled leaf inside a tea bag has no such option: it was spent in the first cup.

## The three most common mistakes

**1. Boiling water.** The number one mistake, and the one that produces "I don't like green tea".

**2. Leaving the leaf in.** If you leave it in the cup while you drink, it keeps steeping and ends up bitter. Remove the leaf when the time is up.

**3. Storing it badly.** Green tea is the most sensitive of all: it oxidises with light, air and humidity. Keep it in an airtight, opaque container, away from light and away from strong-smelling spices. Well stored, green tea lasts months; badly stored, it loses aroma in weeks.

## Why green tea is different

Green tea skips the oxidation step that black tea and oolong go through. The leaves are heated soon after harvest to stop oxidation, which preserves the colour and the vegetal profile. That is why it is more delicate — and why it forgives boiling water less: there is less structure to hold up.

That same sensitivity is why, when you brew it well, the sweet, vegetal flavour everyone talks about finally shows up.
