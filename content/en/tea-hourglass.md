## Why it actually helps

Steeping time is one of the two factors that most change the taste of a tea — the other is water temperature. A green tea left past 3 minutes turns bitter, and there is no fixing it afterwards.

The problem is that nobody has a stopwatch at hand when the water goes on. The hourglass solves that with a single decision: which of the three times to use. After that, you just watch it.

It is an object used every day that, unlike one more mug, changes the result of something the other person already does.

## What to look for in a tea gift

This category is full of objects that get used once. Before buying, three questions are worth asking:

**Does it change the result?** A new mug, a coaster or a decorative spoon do not change how the tea turns out. A timer does: steeping time is one of the two variables that define the taste.

**Will they use it without learning anything?** Gifts that depend on a new technique tend to end up on the shelf. A sand timer is understood in two seconds: flip it and watch.

**Does it help if they already have everything?** Someone who has drunk tea for years probably already owns a teapot, an infuser and cups. What they almost never have is a convenient way to measure time — because a tea bag does not need one, and the phone is never near the kettle.

If the answer to all three is yes, the gift will outlast the week.

## Hourglass, phone stopwatch or kitchen timer

There are other ways to measure steeping time. The difference is the friction in each one:

| Method | What actually happens |
|---|---|
| **Phone** | You have to unlock it, find the stopwatch, start it and walk back to the kitchen. The screen goes dark and it often gets forgotten. |
| **Kitchen timer** | It works, but it beeps, needs a battery, and is usually busy with something else. |
| **Remembering "roughly"** | This is what most people do, and it is exactly why the tea turns bitter. |
| **Hourglass** | You flip it and watch. No screen, no battery, no alarm to silence. |

It is not that a phone does not work: it is that the best tool is the one you use every day without thinking.

## How to use it

1. Put the leaf in the infuser and the water at the right temperature.
2. Choose the time based on the tea: 2, 3 or 5 minutes.
3. Flip the hourglass and wait for all the sand to fall.
4. Remove the leaf when it finishes. Done.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should loose leaf tea steep?

It depends on the type: green tea for 2 to 3 minutes, black tea or a blend for around 3, and spice, fruit or herbal infusions for about 5. Past that point the leaf keeps giving, but what it gives is bitterness.

### Does it work with tea bags?

It does, although a bag forgives time less than whole leaf: being milled, it releases everything at once and turns bitter faster. If someone uses bags, a 2 or 3 minute timer avoids the most common mistake.

### Does it need batteries or maintenance?

No. It is a sand timer: it works by gravity. There is no mechanism, no battery, and nothing to calibrate.

### Is it a good gift for someone just starting with tea?

Yes, and it is one of the few that works at both ends: for a beginner it orders the timing from day one, and for someone with years of practice it fixes a problem they probably never bothered to solve.
